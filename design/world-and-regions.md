# Lost Keep — World & Regions (draft v0.1)

Companion to `ability-atlas.md`. Based on the macro map mockup
"Vaelora — Age of the Tether". Times are rough first-playthrough estimates.

## 1. The Blight-Tide (canon summary)

1. **The Sovereign Prism** is Vaelora's life source, the bond between its
   people and the natural order.
2. **The Vexari exploit it.** Through the Prism they extract **Anima** from
   living creatures, mostly Lirien and Rootlings, and consume it to restore
   their youth.
3. **The abuse destabilises the Prism**, and the **Blight-Tide** seeps out
   of it.
4. **The Blight spreads through the ecosystem.** Plants twist, animals mutate
   and turn aggressive, and anything exposed becomes infected, Lirien
   included.
5. **The Vexari try to weaponise it.** **The Alchemist** finds that Blight
   can be turned on their enemies. In the end it threatens everyone, the
   Vexari too.

### What it means in play
- **The map tells it:** Blight veins (crimson-magenta on the map, red tendrils in the world) radiate *out of the Prism* at
  the bottom of the map. A player who looks at the map can see where the
  rot comes from before anyone says it.
- **It spreads by act.** The vein overlay grows at story beats, re-routing
  and re-populating regions you've already cleared. Old regions change
  instead of being repeated.
- **Infected Lirien are enemies.** Some of the missing aren't in cocoons or
  vats; they've turned. Rescues aren't guaranteed, and that's where the
  grief comes from.
- **Kleo isn't immune.** Blight exposure (Blightwater, Blight mist) builds
  up a status effect. Anima Siphon can drain Blight crystals, which is the
  same technology that caused the disaster.
- **The Silk Mother → Hearth-Plains Kindred** (see §1b). She's the Hearth-Plains' own
  great silk-spinner, twisted by Blight. Her cocoons started as a way to *hide*
  the villagers from Vexari harvesters; the Blight turned protection into
  imprisonment. The first boss is a victim, not just a monster.

## 1b. Peoples of Vaelora

### Rootlings
Small, round, cheerful humanoids who farm, cook, sing and dance in
close-knit villages. They're the most innocent people in Vaelora, which is
exactly why their harvesting has to land as horror.
- **In play:** a Rootling village in the Hearth-Plains, met early, warm and
  full of life. At a later Blight act it's **empty**: the cooking pots still
  hot, a song half-written. No exposition is needed.
- **At the Refuge,** rescued Rootlings run the kitchen and garden. They're the
  hub's heart, and food is the healing or buff service.
- **Never an enemy type.** Infected Rootlings can appear *in cocoons or vats*,
  but Kleo never kills one.

### The Alchemist
The Vexari who experimented on the Sovereign Prism and **discovered essence
extraction**. Everything (the vats, the Blight, the weaponised Tide) starts
with them.
- **A recurring presence:** glimpsed early (notes, a figure behind glass at
  the Foundry), fought at the **Anima Vats**.
- **The Alchemist's notes** are a collectible lore trail. The player
  reconstructs the discovery in the order it happened.

### The Seven Kindreds
All Lirien share one ancestry and the ability to meld memories; the biome
shaped each Kindred's body, philosophy and stance toward the Vexari.

| Kindred | People | Map region (was) | Stance / role in Kleo's story |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High-Spire** | Aetheri | High-Spire Scribes | Scholars. Their **High Voice** is flattered and manipulated by the Vexari. Vesper's people |
| **Ironbark** | Vanguard | Ironbark Guard | Martial guardians, pitted against the Hearth-Plains. Kleo's people; the Lost Keep |
| **Deep-Glow** | Lumen | Deep-Glow Caverns | Cave-dwellers who make their own light. Sora's people |
| **Tide-Weaver** | Nautil | *Tideborn* | Water folk |
| **Fen-Stalker** | Mire-Kin | *Thornveil* | Swamp hunters, scorned as "primitive" by High-Spire |
| **Hearth-Plains** | Loam-Born | *Silkbound* | Farmers and silk-growers, neighbours of the Rootlings; distrust Ironbark |
| **Dusk-Drifter** | Ash-Walker | *Ashforge* | Wanderers of the ash wastes; fire-craft (Emberwick) |

**Silkbound → Hearth-Plains:** the spiders stay. The Hearth-Plains farm
silk, and their great silk-spinner, a beloved, almost domestic beast, was
twisted by the Blight. She cocoons villagers (Lirien and Rootlings) to
"protect" them. The cocoon imagery now carries the farm-life loss.

### The Manufactured Divide in play
Each Voice answers to the High Voice, and the Vexari keep the Kindreds
suspicious of one another so nobody notices villagers vanishing.
- **Kleo starts distrusted.** Being Ironbark, she's met coldly in the
  Hearth-Plains and with contempt in the High-Spire. Dialogue and prices
  change as trust is earned.
- **The Refuge is the counter-argument.** Rescued people of every Kindred
  live side by side. Early on they argue and keep to their own corners;
  as rescues mount, the camp **visibly mixes**: shared fires, shared songs.
  The unity the Vexari fear is something the player builds.
- **The Voices are story gates.** Convincing each Kindred's Voice (by
  rescuing their people, or bringing proof through Memory-Meld) opens
  their sanctum, and **uniting the Voices** is the final story gate into
  the Sovereign Prism.
- **The High Voice** is the last Voice to turn: confronted in the High-Spire
  after the Reveal, a believer, not a villain. Possibly a fight, possibly
  convinced, depending on the evidence the player has gathered.

## 2. Map read: what's working

- **Its directions mean something.** North is Vexari power (the Spire), south
  is the source (the Prism), the hub sits on the axis between them; west is
  living nature (Fen-Stalker, Deep-Glow, Tide-Weaver) and east is industry and fire
  (Dusk-Drifter, the Carapax Foundry). The world tells the story on its own.
- **A central hub with a vertical spine:** Spire, Refuge, Vats and Prism are
  one column, the Tether made physical, and a landmark you can't get lost
  around.
- **The long express corridors** across the top (Observatory ↔ Spire ↔
  High-Spire) work naturally as late-game shortcuts.
- **There's a warp in every region and saves are frequent,** which keeps
  backtracking painless.

## 3. Map read: what to watch

1. **The hub touches everything.** A central start with doors in every
   direction needs strong gates, or the opening becomes a free-for-all. Plan
   the opening as Ironbark → Refuge, then **two** open directions (east or
   west), not seven.
2. **Where is the Lost Keep?** It isn't labelled on the map. Proposal: the
   Lost Keep is the **Ironbark Guard fortress**, the opening and the last
   place you take back.
3. **The hub's name gives away the twist.** "Memory-Meld Refuge" names the
   mid-game reveal. Proposal: it's just **"the Refuge"** until Vesper's meld,
   and then the survivors rename it.
4. **Scope:** 15 areas and about 11 bosses comes to about 12–15 h with
   side content. That's great for a full game but heavy for a first slice.
   Build **Ironbark Guard + the Refuge** as the vertical slice.
5. **Many corridors are only one cell wide.** That's fine in a mockup, but
   each one needs an idea (`room-construction`) or it becomes filler.
6. ~~New lore terms~~: resolved in §1b.
7. ~~Name clash~~: resolved. The boss is **the Silk Mother**; *Sovereign
   Matriarch* belongs to the Kindred Voices only.

## 4b. Gate legend (the map's coloured bars)

Each gate colour means exactly one requirement for the whole game, and each
is paired with an icon shape so the map stays readable for colour-blind
players. **Crimson and magenta are reserved for the Blight.**

## Ironbark surface: level-design references (mockups R to W)

The creative lead's surface mockups (`docs/art-production/lostkeep-attach/`
R, S, T; U, V, W for a greener deepwood later) are level-design ideas as well
as art targets. What they share, for the surface rooms:

- **A great tree on one side** whose branches are walkways across the room's
  upper half, lanterns hanging from them, small islands resting on them.
- **A chapel on a hill** opposite, climbed by stair terraces of grass and stone:
  the room's high point and a goal you see from the start.
- **A pool at the lowest point** with a broken bridge and stepping stumps:
  water as the floor of the room (and a Deep-Glow Breath lock below).
- **Floating ruin islands** in the middle air, carrying arches and graves.
- **Three heights read at once:** the branch line, the terrace line, the
  water line, which is the principles' "multiple levels with floors".

Where they show rows of tiny stepping blocks in the air, the principles win:
islands and branch walks, not small platforms.
