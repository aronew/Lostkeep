What the region needs

Ironbark Guard is Kleo's home, emptied: a great ironbark forest, a graveyard, a broken arch bridge over a dark moat, and the fortress of the Ironbark Guard, the Lost Keep. It is also where the player meets the lock of almost every later ability, so it has to be read at a glance and remembered. Nothing is built yet, so every piece starts as a need.

Piece in the regionTodayStatus

There are no characters, enemies or HUD in this book. Kleo, the Carapax scouts and the HUD get their own books. Water, fog, light, the vignette and the Memory-Meld outline are drawn by the game, not painted.

How to use this book

Start with the pilot: IB1, IB2 and P3.

Those three decide the look of every room. Make the rest only once they read well in a test room in Godot.

  1. Attach the images each card lists. They are in docs/art-production/lostkeep-attach/. Image A, the Ironbark mockup, goes with every card.
  2. Press Copy and paste the prompt into your image model. The copy includes the shared rules unless you untick the box at the top.
  3. Check the result against the checklist. If it falls short, use a fix from If the image falls short.
  4. Save it under the card's file name and hand it to me, exactly as the generator made it. Don't run it through another cleanup or export tool first: one rebuilt the first crypt kit's shapes and snapped its colours, and it lost what made it look painted. I reduce it to true pixel scale, snap it to the palette, cut it, and set it up in Godot.
  5. Tick it done. Ticks are saved in this browser only.

How the art is assembled

The art is skin on a skeleton. These rules are why the cards ask for what they ask for.

Black box first

  • Every room is blocked out as solid black masses (#0B0812) with flat floors and ceilings. That is the collision.
  • Terrain art is painted onto the edges of those masses. The inside of each mass is the same black, so the box itself becomes the shadow and the depth: every edge piece is lit along its open edge and steps down into that black like a shadow. A mass is the nearest thing to the camera, so it is black at its heart and only its edges carry art.
  • So every walking line is straight and exact, but nothing else is: faces, corners and undersides are organic, and each band ends in the void along its own stones and roots, never along a straight line or an even fade. Art may overhang the collision by a few pixels.

Floors, not platforms

  • Height comes from big masses and storeys of floor, plus a few floating islands that read as landforms.
  • Small platforms are rare and disguised: a timber walkway, a root, a fallen beam.
  • So the kit is mostly masses, bands and islands, with only one platform card.

Parallax camera

  • Camera2D with Parallax2D layers: sky 0.05 · far 0.15 · mid 0.35 · back wall 0.7 · playfield 1.0 · foreground 1.3.
  • Each far layer is lower in contrast and bluer, so depth reads at once.
  • Every parallax layer repeats left to right without a seam.

Light by the engine

  • CanvasModulate darkens the scene; lanterns are PointLight2D; there is a screen vignette and layered fog.
  • So the art paints the flame and the lit glass, never the halo around them.
  • Ambient light in the art is soft and from above, the same on every piece.

Prompts

Crypt stone after reference Y (the tile set)

The creative lead's pick for the crypts' stone: the fine grey-lavender brick of reference Y, with its pale cap row and its masses stepping down into darker plum masonry before the black. It replaces SH1 and SH2 (the chunky blue-grey blocks) as the terrain of IB-02 and IB-05; the SH islands, shrine and props stay unless they clash. Make CY1 first; every later card attaches it. These are terrain pieces painted onto black-box collision: walking lines perfectly straight, every piece joining its neighbours, sizes in the room's grid (one tile = 16 px in game = 128 px at x8).

Every piece fades into the dark gradually. The creative lead's pick from a generated CY set: broken slabs whose stone runs on under the walking line and steps down brick by brick, lavender grey to dusky plum to near-black, loose bricks crumbling at the edge, then black. That rule (FADE in every card) is what makes a mass read as solid and deep; a straight line between light and dark, a flat black slab or a hard cut is a fail. CY6 is a set of big masses built around it.

Prompts

Foreground depth (the nearest layers)

The layers in front of Kleo, nearest the camera, that give a room its depth: they scroll faster than the playfield (1.3 to 1.5) and frame the view like the dark roots and pillars at the edges of mockup Y. Today they are placeholders (the crypt's OC7 strip, and P5's ferns blown up into flat black shapes). These are near-black silhouettes, but painted ones: lit faintly at their rims by the room's own light, never flat black cut-outs. They must be sparse: big shapes with wide gaps, so Kleo is behind them only for a moment. FG1 and FG2 are for the crypts (IB-02, IB-05), FG3 and FG4 for the surface (IB-03, IB-04).

Depth in value steps (the creative lead, after Y and Hollow Knight): between the ground Kleo stands on and the black silhouette there are one or two nearer layers of rubble and stone, each a step darker and scrolling a little faster: the playfield (full value, 1.0), near rubble (about 70 % value, 1.12), nearer rubble (about 45 %, 1.25), then the black silhouette (1.4). FG5 is painted for those middle steps: lit like the playfield, so the game can darken each copy to its step without it going muddy.

Prompts

Character: what the crypt rooms hang on

After the creative lead's ten mockups ("they have so much character to the level", docs/mockups/character/; AA and AB are two of them). What gives them their character is not the brick but what is built from it: no bare right angle anywhere. Every slab has a thick underside that tapers into hanging stone with moss draped off it; floors are held up by columns, corbels and arches instead of floating; slopes and corners are stepped course by course; every walking top carries something (crystals, rubble, pots, a candle); alcoves with arches and a lantern are rooms inside the room; the room's outer rock is a rounded cobble mass wound with roots, not a rectangle.

One departure from the mockups, on purpose: their roots are crimson, which is the Blight's colour alone (here they are dull bark-brown and plum). Their floating slabs are welcome: platforms belong in the mix with large masses and large floating islands, as in these mockups; what the principles forbid is filling a room with dozens of them to cover space. Every card uses the CY brick, pixel grid and fade rule, so the pieces sit on the terrain without a seam.

Check every result

Every image

  • Put it next to image A. It looks like the same artist made both.
  • It is real pixel art: hard square pixels on one even grid, no blur, no soft gradients, no anti-aliased edges.
  • No text, labels, grid lines, swatches or watermarks.
  • Transparent where the card says so, with no checkerboard or white fringe.
  • No crimson tendrils, red roots or magenta: those belong to the Blight alone.

Terrain and masonry

  • The walking line is dead straight: lay a ruler on it.
  • The band's far side ends in plain void colour along the stones' own outlines: no straight line or even fade meets the void.
  • Faces and corners are organic and worn; only walking lines are straight.
  • No two stones or roots look copied.
  • Fill textures stay dark and quiet when tiled 3 × 3.
  • Island tops are flat; only their undersides are organic.

Parallax

  • Each layer is darker or hazier than the one in front of it.
  • The left and right edges join.
  • The Spire is visible but small on P2: a landmark, not a subject.
  • P5 is nearly black, so it frames rather than distracts.

Landmarks, props and locks

  • Every lock in the gates group reads at game size, with its one colour cue.
  • Lanterns have a flame but no halo.
  • Each sheet's pieces are separated by clear transparent space.

Seams and palette do not need to be perfect. I snap the palette and repair small joins when processing, so judge the painting first.

What happens next

  1. Godot project: I set up the Godot 4.7.2 project: pixel-perfect 640 × 360 view, the black-box TileMapLayer with terrain autotiling, the parallax camera, lighting and the vignette.
  2. Pilot room: IB1, IB2 and P3 go into one test room, black box first, and you judge it in the game.
  3. Full kit: once you approve, you make the rest and the whole Ironbark kit goes in.
  4. Then the world: with the kit ready, Ironbark's rooms are blocked out from the lock-and-key registry, one approved room at a time.
